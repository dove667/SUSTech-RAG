\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Motivation}

University websites are the primary channel through which institutions communicate academic, administrative, and campus-life information to students and staff. At the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), this content is distributed across hundreds of web pages covering departmental announcements, administrative procedures, facility information, and event notices. Traditional keyword search returns a ranked list of pages and leaves the user to read and synthesise the answer themselves. This places the burden of information extraction on the user and produces poor results for questions that span multiple pages or require understanding natural language context rather than matching terms.

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated the ability to understand and generate fluent natural language across a wide range of tasks. However, a general-purpose LLM trained on internet-scale data has no reliable knowledge of institution-specific details---current staff contacts, procedural requirements, room assignments, course announcements---that change frequently and were either absent from or underrepresented in training corpora. Querying such a model directly produces responses that are fluent but factually unreliable.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \parencite{rag2020} addresses this gap by combining the generative capability of LLMs with the factual grounding of document retrieval. Rather than relying on parametric memory, a RAG system retrieves relevant passages from a curated knowledge base at query time and conditions generation on that evidence. This makes the system's knowledge updatable by re-indexing documents, traceable through source citations, and controllable through the scope of the indexed corpus.

This project builds a local-first RAG system for SUSTech's public web content. ``Local-first'' means the entire pipeline---embedding, retrieval, reranking, and language model inference---runs on local hardware without reliance on external APIs, ensuring data privacy and full operational control. The system is designed to handle the practical challenges of real-world campus content: mixed Chinese and English text, structured announcement templates, short informational pages, and a heterogeneous crawl of several hundred pages from across the SUSTech website.

\section{System Overview}

The system consists of two independent phases. In the \emph{offline indexing phase}, publicly available pages are crawled from the SUSTech website, cleaned to remove navigation boilerplate and low-quality content, segmented into semantically coherent chunks, and encoded as dense vectors using BGE embedding models \parencite{bge2024}. The resulting chunks and their embeddings are persisted in a ChromaDB \parencite{chromadb} vector store alongside a BM25 index for sparse retrieval.

In the \emph{online inference phase}, a user query triggers a multi-stage pipeline. Hybrid retrieval combines dense nearest-neighbour search with BM25 sparse retrieval, fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) \parencite{rrf2009}. A cross-encoder reranker refines the candidate set. A self-RAG reasoning loop \parencite{selfrag2024} then uses the language model itself to assess whether the query is in scope, whether each retrieved chunk is genuinely relevant, and whether the generated response is factually supported by the evidence. Generation is handled by Qwen3 \parencite{qwen2025}, running on either llama.cpp \parencite{llamacpp} or vLLM \parencite{vllm2023} depending on the deployment environment. The response is streamed token-by-token to a Vue~3 frontend interface via Server-Sent Events.
